Anomaly Summary
Radio quality collapses across the window — SNR and CQI dip and MCS flattens — so the air interface is struggling rather than congested by traffic.
Uplink and downlink throughput stay very low while retransmissions climb and a few downlink errors appear, which is the signature of a sick RF link.
Traffic volume is modest and steady with no bursty surges or scheduler pressure you’d expect from a volumetric event.
LIME/SHAP point to persistently low downlink bitrate and elevated downlink retransmissions as the dominant drivers of the decision.

Misclassification Likelihod
Likely false positive for DDoS. 
The model is keying off “low bitrate + high re-tx,” but in context those are fully explained by poor RF conditions.
A real flood would usually bring bursty tx patterns, asymmetry, or control-plane churn—none of which show up here.

Mitigation Step
Pull cell/sector RF KPIs for this interval (CQI/MCS, interference, coverage) and check neighbor relations/handovers to confirm a local radio issue; remediate if persistent.
Put a short watch on this UE/sector: continue tracking uplink/downlink retransmissions and error rates; escalate only if they rise while RF recovers or if the pattern spreads to multiple UEs
Correlate with backhaul/policy logs to rule out shaping or drops; if doubt remains, take a brief packet capture at the edge to verify the traffic mix.
